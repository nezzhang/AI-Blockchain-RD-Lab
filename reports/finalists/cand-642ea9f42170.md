## Executive Summary

## Problem

Continuous AMM-style FX lets arbitrage bots extract spread value from every crossing trade, and thin currency pairs suffer the worst pricing exactly where cross-border payments need them; existing DEX architectures monetize this extraction rather than eliminating it.

## Mechanism

Batch auction pricing eliminates intra-batch MEV: with one uniform clearing price per corridor per batch, there is nothing to front-run WITHIN a batch, and cross-corridor composition gives thin pairs the liquidity of the deepest adjacent path. The rebate-pool surplus recycling removes the operator's fee incentive to widen spreads. Causal chain: orders accumulate in a batch → single clearing price per corridor (path composition allowed) → surplus flows to the next batch's rebates → thin corridors merge upward when volume-starved → liquidity concentrates instead of fragmenting.

## Mathematical Model

- `X_t` (input, dimensionless): Exogenous flow-shock series from the simulation harness: positive = excess buy pressure on the corridor.
- `V_t` (state, dimensionless): Net corridor order imbalance in the current batch (signed volume).
- `P_t` (state, dimensionless (reference = 1.0)): Corridor clearing price relative to its reference level.
- `S_t` (state, dimensionless): Batch surplus accumulated into the next batch's rebate pool.
- `R_t` (state, dimensionless): Rebate pool level available to the next batch's participants.
- `L_t` (output, dimensionless): Corridor liquidity index: volume relative to the merge floor.
- `V_t1` (state, dimensionless): Next batch's order imbalance.
- `P_t1` (state, dimensionless (reference = 1.0)): Next batch's clearing price.
- `L_t1` (state, dimensionless): Next batch's liquidity index after merge decision.

- `V_t1 = V_t * 0.5 + shock_load * X_t` — Next batch's imbalance: half the current imbalance persists (correlated flow) plus the exogenous shock.
- `P_t1 = clip(P_t * (1 + k_price * V_t), 1 - p_band, 1 + p_band)` — Uniform clearing price moves with net imbalance, bounded per batch by p_band — the price-stability property of batch auctions.
- `S_t = k_surplus * abs(V_t) * (1 + k_shade)` — Batch surplus from crossed limit spreads; the k_shade term models the solver-shading channel explicitly so the battery can measure its cost.
- `R_t = R_t * (1 - rebate_share) + S_t` — Rebate pool accumulates surplus and pays out rebate_share per batch.
- `L_t1 = max(1, merge_gain) * min(1, abs(V_t1) / v_floor) + (1 - min(1, abs(V_t1) / v_floor)) * merge_gain` — Liquidity index: above-floor volume yields full index 1; below-floor volume triggers the merge uplift (routing via the parent path) so thin corridors borrow deep-path liquidity.
- `P_t1 = P_t1 * L_t1` — Cross-corridor path composition: when a corridor borrows parent-path liquidity (L_t1 > 1 via merge), the effective clearing price reflects the composed route's depth.

**Parameters:** k_price ∈ [0.01, 2.0] (default 0.3), p_band ∈ [0.001, 0.1] (default 0.02), k_surplus ∈ [0.0, 1.0] (default 0.5), rebate_share ∈ [0.05, 1.0] (default 0.25), v_floor ∈ [0.05, 1.0] (default 0.3), merge_gain ∈ [1.0, 5.0] (default 2.0), shock_load ∈ [0.0, 1.0] (default 0.5), k_shade ∈ [0.0, 1.0] (default 0.1)

**Open questions (§13):** Does the k_shade solver-shading term accumulate into a systematic rebate-farming channel across batches, or does rebate competition self-limit it? (The battery measures this.); What batch cadence balances MEV suppression against merchant latency tolerance — is 30 seconds right, or should hybrid instant/batch flows exist?; The merge uplift merge_gain=2 is a guess; what does composed-path pricing actually cost in practice?; No un-merge rule: once a corridor merges, when (if ever) does it stand alone again? The ratchet concern from the economist report is unresolved.; How does the mechanism behave when multiple corridors shock simultaneously — is cross-corridor composition stable under systemic events?; Statistical confidence: k_price and k_surplus ranges are priors, not measurements; the battery's sensitivity sweep should identify which regimes are safe.

**Critical assumptions:** All orders within a batch clear at one uniform price per corridor (the frequent-batch-auction property).; Solver surplus-shading is linear in spread size and captured by k_shade — a modeling simplification of a strategic behavior.
